### 09 · Aircraft Leasing Strategy and Buy vs. Lease Evaluation

**Goal:** Assess the financial and operational implications of leasing versus purchasing aircraft. Determine the most cost-effective approach to fleet acquisition, optimizing capital expenditures and aligning with growth and demand forecasts.

**Data Required:**
- Current fleet composition (owned vs. leased)
- Lease terms (duration, renewal options, costs)
- Purchase costs for new and used aircraft, including financing rates
- Estimated residual value of owned aircraft
- Maintenance and operating costs for leased vs. owned aircraft
- Aircraft depreciation rates and tax benefits
- Demand forecasts for long-term fleet requirements
- Cash flow forecasts and capital availability

**Key Formulas:**
```
TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) = Purchase Price + Total Maintenance Costs + Depreciation - Residual Value
Total Lease Cost              = Monthly Lease Payment × Lease Term + Maintenance Costs
```

**Methodology:**
1. Analyze Current Fleet Composition
2. Calculate Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)
3. Calculate Total Cost of Leasing
4. Assess Flexibility Needs (demand forecasts and route changes)
5. Evaluate Cash Flow and Capital Availability
6. Consider Residual Value and Depreciation
7. Compare Operational Costs: Leased vs. Owned
8. Scenario Testing for Buy vs. Lease under various market conditions

**Output Format:**
- Fleet Composition Summary
- TCO and Lease Cost Comparison Table
- Flexibility and Cash Flow Analysis
- Scenario Analysis Results

**Improvement Steps:**
1. Optimize fleet mix (balance owned and leased to align with demand)
2. Include purchase options in lease agreements
3. Prioritize ownership of high-residual-value aircraft
4. Negotiate favorable lease terms (lower maintenance costs, flexible renewals)
5. Monitor fuel prices, interest rates, and demand trends to re-evaluate strategy
